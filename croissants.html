Croissants

  • Pastry
  • French
  • Baking
2 days, mostly restingMakes 12

Flaky, buttery croissants laminated by hand over two days, with a honeycomb crumb inside.

Ingredients

  • 500g bread flour
  • 55g sugar
  • 10g salt
  • 10g instant yeast
  • 140ml cold milk
  • 140ml cold water
  • 305g unsalted butter: 25g soft for the dough, 280g cold for the block
  • 1 egg, beaten, for the wash

Method

  1. Mix the flour, sugar, salt, yeast, milk, water and soft butter into a smooth dough, about 5 minutes. Press it into a flat rectangle, wrap it and chill overnight.
  2. Next day, bash the cold butter between two sheets of baking paper into a 19cm square. Chill it until firm but still bendy.
  3. Roll the dough into a rectangle twice the size of the butter. Lay the butter on one half, fold the dough over and pinch the edges shut.
  4. Roll it out to about 60cm long, then fold it in thirds like a letter. Chill for 30 minutes. Do this twice more for three folds in total.
  5. Roll the dough 4mm thick and cut long triangles about 9cm across the base. Stretch each one gently, then roll up from the base.
  6. Proof on lined trays somewhere around 25°C until they wobble when the tray is shaken, 2 to 3 hours. Any warmer and the butter starts to leak.
  7. Brush with egg and bake at 200°C for 5 minutes, then at 180°C for 15 to 20 minutes until deep brown all over.

Notes

The butter and dough need to be the same firmness when you roll. If the butter cracks, let it sit out for a few minutes; if it starts to smear, put everything back in the fridge.
